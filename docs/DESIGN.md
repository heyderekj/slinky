# Slinky — design principles and references

Read this before any design work. Every visual decision should trace back to something here. If it doesn't, decide on purpose and add it.

## Principles
1. **Intentional and tasteful over busy.** Each screen has one job and one primary action. When in doubt, remove.
2. **Native Tahoe first.** Use system components (`NavigationSplitView`, inspector, toolbar, sheets, glass buttons) before inventing. Custom only where Slinky has something to say: the `.env` view, recovery-code chips, Quick Access, approval cards.
3. **Glass on chrome, paper under values.** Liquid Glass belongs to sidebars, toolbars, floating panels, menu bar cards and toasts. Secret values always sit on opaque cool paper (`Color.paper`), in monospace, at 4.5:1 contrast or better. Never glass on glass.
4. **Glacier cyan.** The brand is clear, glassy and white, with glacier cyan (Derek, 2026-10-17). It replaced ice blue, which sat on the same hue as Harvous; the Testament Made suite already had blue (Harvous), indigo (Dinky), pink (Binky), amber (ReviseMy) and green (Koati), and cyan was the open spot that still says ice. Accent `#0b7f8f` in both light and dark mode, used once per view on the primary action. It's deep enough for white text (about 4.7:1), which macOS uses on sidebar selections, menus and prominent buttons. Cyan stays in the icon, backdrop and tints. No saturated color in UI chrome.
5. **The family way of teaching.** Like Dinky and Binky, there's **no welcome screen and no wizard**. The app opens into itself. Empty states teach by doing ("No items yet. Add your first item with ⌘N."), and setup happens in context (the vault is created on the first save, and the Recovery Key appears right then as a sheet).
6. **Calm motion.** Springs, not bounces for their own sake. Motion explains a change (things slink in and out). Everything honors Reduce Motion.
7. **Plain, serious copy.** See `VOICE.md`. Clear and kind for everyone, non-technical first, no clever labels or wordplay in the app, no exclamation points, no fear.

## Before shipping any UI change
- Check light and dark, both ends of the macOS 27 transparency slider, Reduce Transparency, Increase Contrast and Reduce Motion.
- Check on a non-Retina or LCD screen if possible. Glass reads worse there.
- Keyboard only: can you do it without the mouse?
- VoiceOver labels on anything masked ("Hidden value, ends in 4f2a").
- Does it still look like the same family as Dinky and Binky?

## Decisions already made
- **Icon:** a glass asterisk with a rounded keyhole (K2, enlarged) on glacier cyan. Real Liquid Glass via `Slinky/AppIcon.icon` (asterisk layer with glass on, keyhole layer on top, automatic gradient `#1ac8db`; keyhole `#072a31`→`#11505b`). Was glacier blue `#4DA3DC` in 1.0.0. Sources and generators in `design/icon/`.
- **Window:** transparent title bar, toolbar background hidden, no opaque title strip. The window itself is glass (`WindowGlass`, 2026-10-17): the desktop shows through, blurred, under a light ice tint. Screens don't paint their own backgrounds over it. Values still sit on opaque paper: the project `.env` is a paper sheet floating on the glass. The lock curtain stays opaque, so nothing shows through while it covers secrets. Reduce Transparency makes the glass solid (macOS does this).
- **Sidebar toggle:** our own button in the content's part of the toolbar (`.navigation`), like Dinky, with the built-in one removed (`.toolbar(removing: .sidebarToggle)`). The built-in toggle sits in the sidebar's part of the toolbar, which is too narrow for it mid-animation, so macOS flashed the overflow » next to the lock switch. View › Show/Hide Sidebar (⌃⌘S) still works.
- **Toolbar:** one control, the lock switch, pinned to the right end of the title bar as an `NSTitlebarAccessoryViewController` (`LockSwitchAccessory`), not a toolbar item, so it never slides into the overflow (») menu while the sidebar animates. It carries its own glass capsule. The switch is a small two-position switch (open lock | closed lock) whose knob sits on the current state. It's always there, on the lock screen too. Before anything is saved it sits on unlocked, and clicking it says "Slinky can lock once you've saved your first item."
- **Locked window** (`LockView`, `LockStage`, `LockCurtain`; Derek, 2026-10-17): the same window as unlocked, with the vault closed. Same toolbar (sidebar toggle and + shown but resting, title "Slinky", subtitle "Locked"), same content surface (an inset list's background), no sidebar. The app icon is the lock, with a still ring of random hex characters around it like ciphertext (never anything from the vault; it scrambles only while Touch ID asks or during the transition). Unlocking: the asterisk turns 60° like a dial, the ring scrambles, then the icon's keyhole opens out (exponentially, from a point) until the whole window is the app, in about 0.6 s. Locking runs it backwards, closing in to the keyhole before the vault locks, so nothing secret is drawn outside it. Reduce Motion skips it. One chrome change per transition, never an animated sidebar (the split view's column animation stuttered over the keyhole): unlocking shows the app's own toolbar and sidebar from the first frame, so the keyhole only reveals a window already at rest; locking switches the toolbar to the locked one as the keyhole starts closing, and the sidebar goes once the curtain covers everything. App icons for the catalog's known apps are loaded in the background at launch, so the first unlock never stalls on them.
- **Layouts:** see `docs/slinky-layouts.html`. A = project `.env` view, B = Library shelf, C = Quick Access (default), E = Rising Shelf (challenger), D = agent receipts, F = menu bar stack, G = widget.
- **Hotkey:** ⌥⌘K for Quick Access (not ⌃⌥Space, not ⇧⌘Space).
- **Sidebar:** a standard macOS list, not tiles. Derek rejected the Reminders-style tiles (2026-10-16).
  - Search field at the top (searches names, never values). Our own `SidebarSearchField`, not `.searchable`: the built-in one moves into the toolbar when the sidebar hides and overflows (») during the animation. ⌘F focuses it, Esc clears it.
  - The top group (no heading, like Passwords and Notes; Derek dropped "Library" 2026-10-17): All Items, Keys & Tokens, Recovery, with SF Symbols and quiet counts. **Needs Attention** (orange) appears at the end only when something needs a person to act: codes running low, a project missing variables, a key expiring within 14 days. Each row says what to do and has a Show button.
  - Projects: a monogram per project; the count turns into an orange "2 missing" when `.env.example` lists variables Slinky doesn't have. Drop a .env file on a project to import it, or a folder to link it.
  - **Adding from anywhere** (Derek, 2026-10-17, replacing "no Add Item in the toolbar"): a + in the main window's toolbar on every view (in a project it adds a variable there), ⌘V in the window when no field is being typed in, and dropping text on the window all open Add Item. Plus ⌘N, the empty-state button, the File menu and the menu bar. Projects: a labeled "New Project" button in the sidebar footer (⇧⌘N), like Notes' "New Folder". No other stray + buttons in headers or footers.
  - With no projects, the section shows a small card: a preview of a .env with hidden values, one line about what a project is, and a drop zone for a folder or .env file.
- **Naming:** Keys & Tokens, Recovery (Recovery Codes and Recovery Keys inside it), Projects, Add Item. No "Kept", "Tuck It Away" or "Secure Notes". Passwords and general notes belong in Apple Passwords and Notes, not Slinky (2026-10-16).
- **Empty states:** `VaultIdleAnimation`, Slinky's sibling to Dinky's IdleAnimation. A secret rises from the bottom edge, its characters turn into dots, it slides into the icon, the keyhole closes, the asterisk turns, and the keyhole returns with a draw-on checkmark. It plays four examples, one of everything Slinky saves (an API key, a project variable where only the value after `=` is hidden, recovery codes, a recovery key), and ends, like Dinky, on a resting collection: the same glass pills, level, in a loose hand-placed scatter around the icon (not a grid or rows). Hover replays it; Reduce Motion shows only the resting collection. The keyhole never rotates: only the asterisk turns (60°, so it lands identical). Below it: a title, one line, one button. Nothing more.
- **SF Symbol animations: only where something changes state.** `.replace` for swaps (Copy → checkmark, lock ↔ unlock), `.drawOn` for completion (the saved checkmark, a used code), `.pulse` while waiting for Touch ID. No bounces or wiggles on selection, detection, warnings or toasts: Derek found them too much (2026-10-16).
- **Small moments** (2026-10-22), all off or plain fades with Reduce Motion: toasts slide up and fade; a used code is crossed off with a line drawn left to right, then its checkmark; counts roll (`.numericText`); a saved item slides into the list and a fixed leak fades out of Health; the Health ring draws in segment by segment; the mark turns one step on the Recovery Key sheet (a vault was just made) and on click in Settings › About; the empty project's little .env masks its example values. Still on purpose: errors, deletes, LIVE, warnings, the wallet refusal.
- **Filled SF Symbols everywhere** (Derek, 2026-10-17): `.symbolVariant(.fill)` is set once at each root (main window, Settings, Help, menu bar, Quick Access), so every symbol uses its filled variant where one exists. Write the plain name in code; the root fills it.
- **Service icons** (`ServiceIcon`, `IconResolver`, 2026-10-17): the person's own choice (Choose Icon…), else the service's Mac app icon if installed, else its bundled logo (Simple Icons, CC0, white or near-black glyph on its brand color in the monogram squircle), else a website icon if one was loaded, else the monogram. Matching uses the link's site, the detected provider, the name and "from", never the value; Apple Intelligence (macOS 26, on-device) guesses names the catalog doesn't know, from the name, "from" and link site only. Website icons are off by default (Settings › General › Load icons from websites), go straight to the site over https with no cookies, and are stored inside the vault. The catalog lives in `design/services/catalog.txt`.
- **Window and sidebars** (2026-10-17): opens at 1100 × 680, shrinks to 880 × 560. Left sidebar 220–320 wide (opens ~250); the width modifier goes last on the sidebar view, or macOS ignores it and lets the sidebar shrink to 140. The right sidebar (details) is our own glass panel (`DetailsPane.swift`) drawn like the left one, not the system `.inspector`: on Tahoe the inspector counts its width twice when sizing the content, which squeezed the left sidebar and pushed it off the window's left edge. Each screen's content can shrink to 300 points. Keep toolbar subtitles short (counts, not descriptions): they don't truncate, and a long one widens the content column.
- **Library (layout B)** (2026-10-17): a compact `List` (one ~44pt line per item: icon, name, a quiet second line, the short peek, LIVE/expiry tags, Copy always visible; codes show "8 of 10 left" with Copy Next) and an `.inspector` with the details: reveal, Copy and Copy as NAME=…, note, link with Open, dates, the code grid, Print, Edit and Delete. Context menu on the whole row, selecting shows the details on the right, double-click opens Edit, ⌘C copies the selection, Delete moves it to Recently Deleted. Replaced the card shelf, which was too tall to scan.
- **Sort and filter** (library toolbar, 2026-10-17): sort by Date Added (newest first, the default, remembered), Name, Service or Expiry (codes: fewest left first). Show: Everything, Live Keys, Expiring Soon (30 days), Running Low (codes), only the ones that fit the view, not remembered, reset when switching views; the icon fills while a filter is on and the subtitle says what's shown.
- **Agents and the command line** (2026-10-17): Settings › Command Line & Agents installs `~/.local/bin/slinky` (a script that runs the app's binary with `--slinky-cli`), adds the zsh hook, and connects AI tools with a plain preview, a backup and Disconnect. Touch ID prompts name who's asking and the exact command. **Activity** (sidebar, only once there's history) is names only, grouped by day.
- **Health** (2026-10-17) replaces Needs Attention and is always in the sidebar (the same heart icon, amber with a count when something needs you, plain when selected). Calm by default (2026-10-22): neutral tiles, amber only on a tile that found something, cyan for clear, the count in plain ink; the ring's segments draw in one after another: Secrets outside Slinky (Check Now, fixes: Move into Slinky, Remove Lines…, Show), Needs you, Tidy up (unused 60+ days, saved twice).
- **Files** (2026-10-17): a group in Keys & Tokens and All Items for .p8/.pem/.p12/JSON secrets; App Store Connect keys carry Issuer ID and Key ID and offer Save a notarytool Profile.
- **Rotation and hosts** (2026-10-17): "Make New Key on <service>…" in the details; Project › Share › Send to Host… with a names preview, compare and Touch ID.
- **Optional helpers** (Settings › General › Helping out, both off): save-from-clipboard banner, reminders.
- **Drafts:** Add Item keeps what's typed if it's closed without saving (memory only, never written anywhere, cleared on lock). Closing with content asks "Discard this item?" with Keep Editing as the default. Edit asks the same about unsaved changes.
- **Recently Deleted:** deleting never confirms; it moves the item to Recently Deleted with Undo in the toast. Items stay 30 days. The sidebar row shows only when it isn't empty.
- **Backups:** File › Export Backup… (Touch ID, then a save panel; named by date only) writes the encrypted vault without this Mac's Touch ID key, so it opens only with the Recovery Key. Restore from Backup… asks for that key and replaces this Mac's vault, keeping one `.previous` copy.
- **Monograms look like little Mac app icons:** a continuous squircle, a vibrant top-to-bottom gradient from a curated palette (glacier cyan, sky, mint, indigo, violet, pink, coral, amber), a soft top sheen, a hairline rim and white initials. With no name yet, Slinky's asterisk on glacier cyan. Examples in the app use "Slinky" as the project name.
- **Sounds** (`design/sounds/make_sounds.py` → `Slinky/Sounds/*.caf`, 6–10 KB each; Derek, 2026-10-17): literal and secure, never cartoon (a first springy "sproing" version was too cartoony). The coil: the soft, fast ripple of tiny metallic ticks a Slinky's coils make settling against each other. The latch: a damped mechanical click with a low body and no ringing tone. Saved = a short coil settle, then the latch (also when a key is replaced). Clear = only the coil, soft and short, when Check Now finds nothing (never the daily check). Imported = a longer ripple, then the latch. Locked = the coil closes up, then the bolt's two clicks, started with the animation so the bolt lands as the keyhole closes. Unlocked = the latch lets go, then the coil opens out. Replaced the system Pop, Glass and Tink.
- **Status colors** (Derek, 2026-10-22: the system green and orange read as a default UI). Three tokens next to `.paper` in `IceBackdrop.swift`, never raw `.green`/`.orange`:
  - `.attention`, a calm amber (#B8862E light, #E2B565 dark): only where something needs a person (expiring keys, low codes, missing variables, keys left in files). As a tint and a small pill, never a solid fill.
  - `.done`, the glacier cyan accent: checkmarks that confirm something worked (saved, copied, used, all clear).
  - `.danger`, plain red, and rare: LIVE keys, deleting, errors.
  The one exception is the wallet-phrase refusal in Add Item, which keeps a warmer orange so it stands out. Locked isn't a warning (the menu bar dot is gray).
  Toasts have three looks: success (cyan checkmark), information (gray info icon) and removed (gray trash icon, with Undo). Toasts center over the content column, not the whole window.
- **Add Item** (`AddItemView.swift`, 2026-10-17): a floating glass panel (720 wide) over a soft dim, with a frosted layer behind it (`PanelBackdrop`), not a sheet. Never blur the window itself: it makes the layout jump under the toolbar. Raycast's one-field model, Apple Passwords' hide-by-default, and the empty-state pill motion.
  - Empty: one large field on paper ("Paste a key, recovery codes or .env lines", ⌘V keycap), plus "Or choose what you're adding": four equal tiles, each an icon, a name and one short description (two lines of text on purpose, never an accidental wrap), for typing something Slinky can't recognize.
  - On paste the value lands straight away: it becomes a slab on paper with the monogram, an editable name, and the value turning into dots left to right, settling into a short form (`sk_live_••••••••4f2a`). An eye shows it; an × clears it. `.env` pastes show `NAME=••••` rows; codes show a dotted grid; a wallet phrase turns the slab orange and saves nothing.
  - Under it: what Slinky thinks it is ("Looks like a GitHub token."), then the details as chips: where it goes (menu), expiry date, project and environment, website or username. Dashed chips are options you can add; tinted chips hold a choice.
  - Footer: "Encrypted on this Mac", Cancel, and "Save to Keys & Tokens ↵", so you always know where it's going.
- **New Project** (`NewProjectView.swift`, 2026-10-17): the same floating glass panel as Add Item. A big name field with a live monogram, then the project folder: a dashed drop zone ("Drop the project folder here", Choose Folder…) or, once chosen, its path with a git chip. Slinky reads the folder (`ProjectScan`, read-only) and lists what it found:
  - each `.env` file with a checkbox, its variable count and first few names (never values), and an environment chip guessed from the file name (`.env.local` → Local, `.env.staging` → Preview, `.env.production` → Production), which can be changed;
  - an orange "Not in .gitignore" chip on files git could commit, with one plain line of advice (Slinky never edits .gitignore itself);
  - `.env.example` (or .sample/.template/.dist) as the list of expected names, with "Still missing after import: …".
  - The button says what happens: "Create and Import 21 Variables ↵". Dropping a folder or .env file on the sidebar opens this panel with the folder already read.
- **Edit** (`EditItemView.swift`): the same floating panel, fields as label/control rows on paper (`FieldCard`). A value is never shown in full while editing: the current one is a peek, and "Replace…" opens a secure field for the new one. Recovery codes can be replaced with a new set (used marks reset). Delete sits bottom left; it moves the item to Recently Deleted, with Undo. Every item can have a note and a link (https only).
- **Settings** (`SettingsView.swift`): Dinky's Settings chrome. A `Window` scene with a unified title bar showing the pane's name, back and forward buttons, a hidden toolbar background, and a sidebar in sections (Settings: General, Security, Recovery Key; Slinky: About). Grouped forms, 760 × 560. About shows honest stats: secrets kept out of files, times copied, and "Seen by: You".
- **Help** (`HelpView.swift`): Help › Slinky Help, short plain sections on paper cards.
- **Duplicates** (2026-10-17): Add Item and Import compare the pasted value with what's saved, in memory while unlocked (`AppModel.places(of:)`), and say where it already is ("Already saved in Keys & Tokens, as GITHUB_TOKEN."). A warning, never a block. Nothing derived from values is stored for this.
- **Import** (`ImportView.swift`, `Importer` in the core): File › Import… (⇧⌘I), and a quiet "Keep keys somewhere else? Import them…" link on the All Items empty state. Labels stay general and don't name another app (Derek, 2026-10-17); Help lists the formats. Reads `.1pux` (a small in-memory ZIP reader; nothing extracted to disk) or CSV. Keeps API credentials, concealed fields that are clearly service keys, backup codes and recovery keys found in notes. Skips logins, passwords and wallet phrases, and says how many it skipped. The panel lists names and peeks only, with items already in Slinky unchecked, and offers to move the export file to the Trash afterwards (on by default; export files aren't encrypted).
- **Needs Attention** also lists recovery keys with no copy outside Slinky, with Print… and "I Have a Copy" right in the row.
- **Updates** (`Updater.swift`, like Dinky's): a daily check with GitHub's latest release (Settings › General can turn it off). The only other network request is website icons, off by default (Settings › General › Load icons from websites), Check for Updates… in the app menu with the family alerts ("All caught up." / Nice, "Couldn't phone home."), and a glass banner at the bottom of the sidebar. Installs in place so there's no second Open Anyway. Before installing, the download must satisfy `identifier "com.slinky.app" and anchor apple generic and certificate leaf[subject.OU] = "B3U6BXA8KP"`; anything else is refused. Slinky locks before it quits to swap.
- **Menu bar glyph:** asterisk radius 5.2 and stroke 2.6 in an 18 pt canvas: about 12.5 × 13 pt of ink, measured against the system items (Wi-Fi 15 × 11, Spotlight 14 × 13, Control Center 12 × 12). Derek found 7.6/3.6 and then 6.4/3.0 too big (2026-10-17). Dinky (14 pt) and Binky (tray symbol, small scale) follow the same measure.
- **Install window (DMG)** (`design/dmg/make_background.py`, `scripts/make-dmg.sh`): Slinky on the left, Applications on the right, a gray dotted arc between them, and a faint gray Slinky coil arching behind, on a flat soft off-white (`#F7F8FA`). No text, no glass card (Derek, 2026-10-17). 600 × 380, icons 128 pt at (160, 178) and (440, 178), 1x + 2x in one TIFF.
  - Finder's rules, tested: a background picture can't follow light/dark mode; with a picture, icon labels are always black; transparent parts are filled with white even in dark mode. So the background has to be light enough for black labels, and "system background plus a pattern" isn't possible. Only Finder's plain default background follows the mode, and it can't show art.
- **Working state:** the keyhole is absorbed into the asterisk, which spins (see `docs/plan.md`).

## Sequoia and Tahoe (2026-10-17)
- Slinky runs on macOS 15 Sequoia and later, to reach more developers. Liquid Glass is Tahoe-only.
- Every glass surface and glass button goes through `Slinky/Views/Glass.swift`: real glass on Tahoe; on Sequoia, Apple's own standard look (the regular material with a hairline edge, bordered and bordered-prominent buttons). Never fake glass on Sequoia.
- The draw-on symbol transition becomes a fade on Sequoia. Layout, wording and behavior are the same on both.
